After an unequivocal experience of the inefficacy of the subsisting
federal government, you are called upon to deliberate on a new
Constitution for the United States of America. The subject speaks its
own importance; comprehending in its consequences nothing less than
the existence of the UNION, the safety and welfare of the parts of which
it is composed, the fate of an empire in many respects the most
interesting in the world.

It has been frequently remarked that it seems to have been reserved to
the people of this country, by their conduct and example, to decide the
important question, whether societies of men are really capable or not
of establishing good government from reflection and choice, or whether
they are forever destined to depend for their political constitutions on
accident and force.
